## Quick Update

Welcome back, friends. It's been a while.

I have recently changed up this platform and moved from the old domain that I don't know what I will do with yet, but I am excited for what's to come. With this new domain `hutlab.dev` and a fresh look to the website, I am planning to start writing more now that I have settled in a little bit.

## What's Changed?

Ashamed as I am, this new website is my jab at <u>vibe-coding</u>. I wanted to move away from a paid CMS (content management system) platform and move to <u>github pages</u>, which is why this made perfect sense. However, I am a design nerd and user experience matters to me. So I started researching what would be the best way to make a beautiful UI and at the same time keep costs minimal. [Fable 5](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-fable-5-mythos-5) could not come out at a better time so here we are.

I am pretty happy with the results - while this website has multiple branches, let me show you around a few things I am proud of, especially because that involved me having to get into the weeds myself. I had to struggle through a little bit of <u>JavaScript</u> along with a little bit of [NunJucks](https://mozilla.github.io/nunjucks/) - a pretty old, but cool JS templating engine that I did not know anything about. So all in all, a little bit of this, and a little bit of that, and Bob's your uncle. We made it.

---

## Let Me Show You Around!

The website is pretty basic, but I have added a few cool things that were not previously present on [hutgrabber.com](https://hutgrabber.com).

- <u>**All New RSS Feeds**</u>: The site now comes with an RSS page that can be viewed by navigating to the footer of the website. Here's the link - [Hutgrabber RSS Feeds](/feed/) - no sign ups, no login required. If you're one of our regulars, it would be nice to have these posts be incorporated into your favorite RSS reader app.

- <u>**Tag Based Organization**</u>: Tags are generally pretty boring, but there are a few cool ways to make them fun. When I was brainstorming what to do here, [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/) came to mind. I have always loved how Obsidian organizes your notes using [graphs](https://obsidian.md/help/plugins/graph) and I was fascinated by the idea of bringing those into my website. However, the older CMS I used did not really have a way for me to inject different templates without <u>self-hosting</u> the whole project, but with a little bit of *Fable 5 sparkle* I was up and running in no time. Check out the new tags view [here](/tags/).

Other than that, the website is pretty much self-explanatory. It was a great experience building this out - although most of it was not even me. It did teach me a few things about prompting and agentic workflows - which now seem to be the future.
